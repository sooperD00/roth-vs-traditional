# Traditional or Roth? A plan for all seasons

*An essay in progress, written as a letter.*

Erasmus opened *The Praise of Folly* with a letter to Thomas More.
In it he called More *omnium horarum homo*, "a man for all hours."
Robert Whittington's 1520 "a man for all seasons" is usually traced to that line.
I borrow the form and the phrase.
A plan for all seasons holds up in whichever regime a given year turns out to be.

## The four questions

1. **What is my retirement calculator not telling me?**
   The hook, with a real answer attached.
2. **How do I show uncertainty to someone who wants one number?**
   Everyone who presents to leadership meets this problem. The answer needs pictures.
3. **What is a model for, if not the answer?**
4. **What does a human add when AI builds the model?**
   The question that holds the other three.

## Notes toward it

- The plan I started with: four model sessions and about ninety minutes of work. The build took a week.
- Separating mechanisms: in my PhD work, each mechanism dominated in its own regime, and knowing the regime told me which model to trust.
- Squash and stretch: a simple engine shifts outcomes, and path dependence changes their shape.
- Counting outcomes: how many end catastrophic, undesirable, acceptable, good or great, and for whom.
- Lines from my drafts: "people will undercount the number of shocks in their life"; "tax deferral is leverage"; "the breakeven doesn't move; it stops existing."
