The mechanism is two multipliers, one fixed and one growing.

Traditional starts ahead by a fixed 1/(1 − t_now) = 1.481×, because the deferral buys more shares.
Per shock, Traditional sells 1.98× what Roth sells for the same cash. So it loses 1.981 / 1.481 = 1.337× as much of itself per shock.
That second multiplier is the one that moves. The gross multiple runs 1.746 → 1.849 → 1.981 across the three shock sizes, because the withdrawal climbs the bracket table as it grows. Proportional damage per shock goes 1.18× → 1.25× → 1.34×.

A fixed head start against a growing per-shock penalty. Enough shocks at a large enough size consumes it, and $80,000 × 3 is past the line.

Why it's worth writing down

1. It's a qualitative flip, not a shading. Every other result in this project moves a breakeven rate by points. This one changes which account wins, and it does so before any retirement-rate comparison. trad₆₅ is a pre-tax number and roth₆₅ is already after-tax — so on the median path there is no retirement tax rate, including zero, at which Traditional recovers. 295,296 × (1 − t) < 390,411 for every t ≥ 0. The breakeven doesn't move; it stops existing.

2. The deterministic model cannot produce it. With no shocks, roth/trad is pinned at 0.675 forever — D-22 proves it, T7 and T8 assert it. The flip is reachable only through the one asymmetric mechanism in the cube. That's the direct answer to the reviewer question "why not just compare tax rates?", and it's the strongest single piece of evidence that the shock machinery earns its cost.

3. It's a sensitivity result that goes against your own convenience. $40,000 is a named fudge factor (D-39, 1.6× a contribution year). The run exists to test whether that choice is load-bearing. The answer is yes — and not "the number moves 8%," but "the qualitative conclusion is not stable across the plausible range." That's the D-05 treatment applied to a second fudge factor, and it's a better story than a clean sensitivity would have been.

4. It has a plain-English reading. Tax deferral is leverage. The Traditional saver holds a bigger position bought with the government's money, and the 10% penalty plus bracket-climbing is the margin call. Leverage amplifies both directions — that's a sentence a person can use, and it's not in the standard advice.
